FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Wills, estate planning and property finance can raise a lot of questions. Here are answers to some of the ones we hear most.

Home › FAQs

If you don't have a valid will, the law decides how your estate is shared out — which may not match your wishes at all. A will lets you decide exactly who inherits what, and can also cover things like guardianship for children.

A will sets out what should happen to your estate after you pass away. A trust is a separate legal arrangement that holds assets on behalf of someone else, and is often used alongside a will as part of wider estate planning — for example, to protect assets for children or vulnerable family members.

Estate planning means organising your affairs in advance — your will, any trusts, and how your assets are arranged — so that your wishes are clear and your family has as little complication as possible when the time comes.

A commercial mortgage is finance secured against a non-residential property — for example, an office, shop, warehouse or other business premises, whether you'll occupy it yourself or let it out.

Development finance is designed around how building projects actually happen — funds are typically released in stages as the work progresses, rather than as a single lump sum. It's used for everything from single-property renovations to larger new-build schemes.
